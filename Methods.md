
Zooplankton biomass observations have been collected globally for well over 100 years, yet most datasets have been unavailable or difficult to use by the research community. Many of these are small-scale datasets, but when combined, they provide zooplankton observations over a large spatial and temporal scale. In the Australian region (100–170°E, 8–50°S), we compiled 51,412 biomass observations (1932-2026) from 64 different datasets collected *in situ* using 6 different sampling methods, 41 different gear types, and 24 different mesh sizes. These observations were matched to ocean bathymetry (GEBCO), sea surface temperature (MODIS-Aqua, 2002–present), and chlorophyll *a* (Copernicus-GlobColour Bio-Geo-Chemical L4, 1997–present) to provide environmental context. Where an exact temporal match was not possible due to the age of the observation, the monthly climatology was used instead.

Using this dataset of Australian zooplankton biomass, we developed statistical models based on a suite of environmental variables and adjusted for biases associated with differences between instrument types and biomass measurement methods. We used a mixed effects modelling framework and included a range of environmental predictors as fixed effects, while accounting for sampling variability using random effects. Fixed effects included temperature, chlorophyll *a*, depth, time of day, and time of year. Random effects were used to account for variance associated with different gear types and datasets (e.g. nuances associated with a deployment). These statistical models were used to produce spatial maps of zooplankton biomass for the Australian region as a long-term mean and monthly climatologies (2002–2015) at a ~4km (at the equator) resolution.
